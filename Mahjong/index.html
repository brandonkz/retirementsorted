Step by step

Learn enough to sit down and play

You do not need to memorize regional scoring before your first game. First learn the machine. Then learn the tax code.

1

The goal

Build a legal hand, usually four groups plus one pair. A group is a Chow, Pung or Kong.

Example: 2-3-4 Bamboo + 7-8-9 Dots + three Easts + 4-5-6 Characters + a pair of Red Dragons.
Mahjong is very open-minded, provided your tiles arrive in approved groups.
2

The tiles

There are three numbered suits: Characters, Bamboo and Dots. Each runs from 1 to 9. Honor tiles are the four Winds and three Dragons.

🀇🀈🀉🀐🀑🀒🀙🀚🀛🀄
3

The combinations

Chow: three consecutive tiles in one suit. Pung: three identical tiles. Kong: four identical tiles. Pair: two identical tiles.

A pair is the easiest relationship in Mahjong. They agree on literally everything.
4

Your turn

Draw a tile from the wall, then discard one. You are trying to make your hand more connected every turn.

Good beginner habit: keep pairs and connected numbers such as 4-5. Discard isolated tiles that have little chance of joining anything.
5

Calling a discard

You can sometimes claim another player's discard to make a Pung, Kong or Chow. Chows are normally only called from the player immediately before you. Called groups are exposed.

6

Winning

When your 14 tiles form a legal hand, call Mahjong. In this learning version, the first legal hand wins. No advanced fan or point scoring yet.

You can add complicated scoring later, once everyone has become emotionally invested.

How to think

Look for lonely tiles

Pairs are useful. Adjacent numbers are useful. Completed groups are obviously useful. A single honor or edge tile with no support is often your first candidate to discard.

But: Mahjong is situational. A tile that looks weak early may become useful later, and visible discards affect what is safe. The practice coach is teaching shape, not prophecy.
